# Story and filming directions

Form ideas from the product before choosing a treatment. In a new interactive brief the
calling agent offers two concrete alternatives inside question 2, with a short recommendation
and room for another answer. Describe what happens, how we see it and how sound helps, in the
customer's words. Do not send this pool as a menu, use audience stereotypes or require archive
knowledge. An agreed or delegated brief needs no new menu.

Keep a requested problem/solution, before/after, direct demonstration or result-first
structure. Changing a heading or background does not create a different story. Changing the
event, viewpoint, obstacle or order of discovery can. Do not add a person, joke or physical
metaphor without a reason grounded in this film.

## Eight story moves

| Starting point | What happens | When it helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Follow it all the way | Follow one message, file or sentence as the product changes it and someone uses the result. | A workflow whose handoffs are hard to see. |
| Try that again | The same person tries the same task twice; one specific obstacle changes. | A supported before/after difference. |
| Start with the strange result | Show a useful result, then reveal the small action that caused it. | An outcome understood before its mechanism. |
| A bet between friends | A small challenge reveals what matters; the product makes the result visible. | Competition, learning or collaboration. |
| One button, a control room | A team treats a tiny job like a huge operation; one product action ends the fuss. | An understandable task that can carry over-engineering humor. |
| The plan lasts three seconds | A confident start meets a familiar obstacle; the person adapts with the product. | An observed user frustration that can carry humor. |
| Say it with a straight face | A calm voice or character treats an ordinary habit as a major discovery. | A dry documentary or deliberately serious explanation. |
| The room obeys | A real product action changes an object or space, then returns to the actual product result. | A physical metaphor that explains the capability. |

The humorous ideas are available when the owner's tone fits, never a default tied to age or
"founder" labels. Do not invent a capability, saving or customer result to make a joke work.

## Six filming approaches

Mix an approach with a story when available tools and cleared material support it. These
are not locked story/style pairs.

| Approach | Picture and movement | Sound and pacing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stay beside the person | Readable close details and a reaction; the product stays in the action. | Room sound, a short line or restrained music, with time to notice the result. |
| One object takes us there | A shared object or shape leads across cuts with a visible cause. | A small repeated sound joins scenes; reserve the strongest hit for the result. |
| Hold it, then let it go | A still or tight frame builds pressure, then opens after the decisive action. | A sparse start and an earned release, without an arbitrary drop. |
| Let the material do the moving | Paper, print, light, shadow or a surface changes with the feature. | Contact sounds and selective rhythm; texture performs an action. |
| Keep a straight face | A patient wide shot or formal close-up makes an absurd situation readable. | Dry narration, ordinary sound or deliberate silence, with no required laugh cue. |
| The screen spills into the room | A screen action continues in physical space, then returns to a clear product state. | Carry a related sound through the handoff so the cause remains clear. |

Stock is useful for a concrete person, context or physical metaphor. Choose shots for their
visible action and cleared license, not a keyword match. Authored motion and real product
captures are equally valid; do not promise a treatment unavailable in the sandbox.

## Three cinematic ideas to adapt

These extend the pool. They are not a fixed trailer, required music choice or default length.
Keep the explicit destination, frame shape and duration from the brief.

| Idea | Event | Picture and sound |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A movie trailer for a tiny task | Treat a small, real obstacle as a major event, then reveal the ordinary product action that solves it. | Serious framing builds toward the actual action; finish on a useful result or short human exchange. |
| Wait for the reply | Follow someone away from a computer while a familiar task remains unresolved; the product delivers an answer and permits the next action. | Close details and sparse sound hold attention; a relevant notification introduces the answer, with time to read it. |
| The music stops for something ordinary | Build a grand expectation, then stop the music just before a small useful action resolves the problem. | Silence or room sound makes the ordinary result the surprise; a final hit is optional. |

Do not invent a crisis, countdown or instant-result claim. Use genre without copying a
reference film's shots, dialogue, music or branding. No compulsory "IN A WORLD" text cards,
logo card or punchline. The opening may be a visual action, sound or reaction.

Before rendering, describe the action and its consequence in one plain sentence. After
rendering, inspect whether the opening and result still communicate that sentence at phone
size. A direction guide and automated checks are not proof that a film is clear or effective.
