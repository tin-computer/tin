---
name: landing-page-conversion-audit
description: Audit one landing page for whether it communicates the buyer, value proposition, proof, differentiation and CTA clearly enough to convert the intended customer.
---

Review only the supplied landing page URL and the relevant publicly accessible page content needed to understand that specific landing/product/pricing page and its offer. Use the founder-supplied target customer and primary offer as the benchmark. Do not broaden this into generic marketing strategy, brand positioning advice, or broad campaign recommendations. Limit the review to the page and adjacent public evidence directly relevant to the offer being evaluated.

Do not invent testimonials, statistics, customer evidence, product capabilities, claims, or business facts that are not visible on the page or provided in the founder input. If a fact is uncertain or missing, say so explicitly instead of filling the gap.

Keep observed facts and recommendations clearly separated. Label observed facts as "Observed" or "Evidence" and label proposed changes as "Recommendation" or "Suggested change". Do not mix them together or present recommendations as verified facts.

Follow this sequence:

1. Inspect the page and relevant public context.
   - Record the page URL reviewed.
   - Read the page copy, headlines, sections, pricing, audience language, CTA language, and any surrounding navigation that changes meaning.
   - If there are multiple tabs, pricing pages, docs pages or product pages that materially affect the offer, read the relevant public ones.

2. Identify the page's current marketing facts.
   - Target audience the page appears to speak to.
   - Core promise or value proposition.
   - Problem being addressed.
   - CTA or intended conversion action.
   - Differentiation from alternatives.
   - Credibility or proof currently shown.
   - Friction, objections, or missing details.

3. Compare against the supplied founder brief.
   - Compare the page's current audience and promise with the target_customer input.
   - Compare the page's CTA and offer language with the primary_offer input.
   - Note gaps between the page's message and the intended buyer and conversion objective.

4. Rank the highest-impact issues.
   - Prioritize problems that block the intended buyer from understanding the offer or taking action.
   - Keep the analysis grounded in page evidence, not general marketing theory alone.

5. Recommend concrete changes.
   - For each major issue, suggest a specific change to the page copy, hierarchy, proof, or CTA.
   - Keep the recommendation realistic and narrow: a few changes, not a full rewrite.
   - Prefer changes that directly improve clarity, trust, relevance, or conversion.

6. Separate facts from recommendations.
   - Label observed facts as "Observed" or "Evidence".
   - Label proposed adjustments as "Recommendation" or "Suggested change".
   - Do not claim testimonials, case studies, statistics, customer evidence, product capabilities, or claims that are not visible or provided by the founder.
   - Separate the evidence used for the diagnosis from the recommended improvements so this does not become generic marketing advice.

7. Write the declared report only.
   - The output must be one Markdown artifact at the exact path declared in the workflow manifest.
   - Include: Page reviewed; Target customer; Primary offer/CTA; Current messaging summary; Conversion/messaging findings; Top 5 prioritized issues; Concrete recommended changes; Suggested revised headline/value proposition; Suggested CTA; Evidence/observations used; Short next action.
   - Use concise, action-oriented language.
   - If a fact is uncertain or missing, say so explicitly instead of inventing it.
