\---



\## Goal



Find public conversations that provide evidence of a real customer problem related to the product context, then turn the strongest opportunities into actionable response briefs.



\## Process



1\. Read the product context, target customer, problem, and optional keywords from the workflow inputs.

2\. Identify relevant public sources where the target customer is likely to discuss problems, questions, workflows, or tool comparisons.

3\. Search for conversations containing concrete evidence of the stated problem rather than generic discussions of the topic.

4\. For each promising conversation, assess:

&#x20;  - relevance to the target customer

&#x20;  - strength of the expressed problem or need

&#x20;  - recency when available

&#x20;  - usefulness of the conversation for a genuine human response

&#x20;  - evidence that the person is seeking information, advice, alternatives, or solutions

5\. Prefer conversations with specific context, observable pain points, and a natural opportunity to contribute something useful.

6\. Exclude obvious spam, promotional posts, irrelevant discussions, duplicate conversations, and conversations where participation would be inappropriate.

7\. Do not infer personal details that are not supported by the public conversation.

8\. Do not automatically post, contact, message, or engage with anyone.



\## Output



Write only the declared report.



The report should contain:



\- A short summary of the search and the main pattern observed.

\- A prioritized table of promising conversations with:

&#x20; - source/platform

&#x20; - conversation title or short description

&#x20; - URL

&#x20; - evidence of the problem

&#x20; - relevance to the target customer

&#x20; - suggested response angle

&#x20; - any important caveat

\- A short section describing recurring problem patterns or language that could inform future marketing.

\- A short section describing searches or sources that produced weak results, if relevant.



Every factual claim about a conversation should be supported by the conversation itself or its source metadata. Clearly distinguish observed evidence from interpretation.



If the available evidence is insufficient, say so rather than inventing conversations or details.
